# Professional communication in English

This unit gives you the English you need for an internship and your first job in tech: writing emails, taking part in meetings, presenting your work and passing a job interview.

## 1. Writing professional emails

A good email has a **clear subject line**, one purpose, and short paragraphs.

| Part | Formal | Neutral / friendly |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting | *Dear Ms Taylor,* / *Dear Hiring Manager,* | *Hi Sarah,* |
| Opening | *I am writing to enquire about…* | *I hope you're well.* |
| Request | *Could you please send me…?* / *I would be grateful if you could…* | *Can you send me…?* |
| Attachment | *Please find attached my CV.* | *I've attached the file.* |
| Closing | *I look forward to hearing from you.* | *Thanks in advance!* |
| Sign-off | *Kind regards,* · *Yours sincerely,* (name known) · *Yours faithfully,* (UK, name unknown) | *Best,* / *Cheers,* |

> [!exemple] Applying for an internship
> **Subject:** Final-year internship application – Software development
>
> Dear Ms Taylor,
>
> I am a final-year computer science student and I am looking for a six-month internship starting in February. I have built several web and mobile applications with Spring Boot and React Native, and I am particularly interested in your work on cloud platforms.
>
> Please find attached my CV. I would be glad to discuss how I could contribute to your team.
>
> Kind regards,
> [Your name]

## 2. Meetings

| Function | Useful phrases |
|---|---|
| Giving an opinion | *In my opinion…* · *I think we should…* · *From a technical point of view…* |
| Agreeing | *I completely agree.* · *That's a good point.* |
| Disagreeing politely | *I see your point, but…* · *I'm not sure that would work, because…* |
| Asking for clarification | *Could you clarify what you mean by…?* · *Sorry, could you repeat that?* |
| Interrupting | *Sorry to interrupt, but…* · *Can I just add something?* |
| Giving a status update | *I've finished the login page.* · *I'm working on the API.* · *I'm blocked by…* |

> [!tip] The daily stand-up
> In agile teams, each person answers three questions: *What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? Is anything blocking me?*

## 3. Presentations

The classic structure: tell them what you'll tell them → tell them → tell them what you told them.

- **Opening:** *Good morning, everyone. Today I'm going to present…* · *My talk is divided into three parts.*
- **Transitions:** *Let's move on to…* · *This brings me to…* · *As you can see on this slide…*
- **Describing data:** *The response time **increased sharply** / **dropped slightly** / **remained stable**.*
- **Closing:** *To sum up…* · *Thank you for your attention. I'm happy to take any questions.*
- **Handling questions:** *That's a great question.* · *I'm not sure, but I'll find out and get back to you.*

## 4. Job interviews

| Question | Tip |
|---|---|
| *Tell me about yourself.* | Present → past → future, in about 2 minutes. |
| *Why do you want to work here?* | Mention something specific about the company. |
| *What are your strengths?* | Give one strength and a short example. |
| *Tell me about a challenge you faced.* | Use the **STAR** method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. |
| *Where do you see yourself in five years?* | Show ambition linked to the role. |
| *Do you have any questions for us?* | Always ask one: *What does a typical day look like for an intern?* |

## 6. Common mistakes made by French speakers

| Don't say | Say |
|---|---|
| *I have 22 years.* | *I am 22 (years old).* |
| *I am agree.* | *I agree.* |
| *Actually* (meaning *actuellement*) | *Currently* / *At the moment* |
| *Eventually* (meaning *éventuellement*) | *Possibly* |
| *I make an internship.* | *I am doing an internship.* |
| *Since two years* | *For two years* |
| *Informations, advices* | *Information, advice* (uncountable) |
| *Can you explain me…?* | *Can you explain to me…?* |
| *I am responsible of…* | *I am responsible for…* |
